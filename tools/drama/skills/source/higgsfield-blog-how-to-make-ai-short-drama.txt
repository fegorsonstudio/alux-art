Step 1: Pick a story people get in seconds

For a short drama, pick a situation people understand quickly. Here are three starting points:

A Cinderella story: someone ordinary gets an unexpected opportunity.
Love against the odds: two people want to be together, but something stands in their way.
A rivalry: two people want the same thing, and only one can get it.

We went with the first one. Our story is about Lucy, an accountant at a fashion house. She interrupts a casting, trips, and catches the director's attention. He thinks she could become the face of the house.

Why one line is enough to generate a scene

You never write a video prompt by hand here. Every clip prompt from the script already carries the decisions a director makes on set:

Locked references: every character and the room are attached by name, with face, hair and outfit matched 100%.
A location map and a first frame
The same voice everywhere:
Scene 1: the hook and the context

The first scene covers two parts of the drama: the hook and the context. The hook starts with the peak of the scene: an emotional outburst, a conflict, a fall. The viewer sees the loudest moment without knowing how it got there, so they keep watching to find out. 
In the context, keep the emotional swings coming every 20 to 30 seconds: a starting point, then hope, then another hit, then a chance.
