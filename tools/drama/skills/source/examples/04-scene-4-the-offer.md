SCENE CONTEXT




Standing face to face in the afternoon sun, the head of the fashion house asks the junior accountant whether she has ever been photographed and offers her the chance to become the face of his next campaign. He is warm with her throughout, careful, almost tender, looking at her the way one looks at something rare. She thinks he is being kind about a mistake and objects that she is an accountant. He steps back, considers her from head to shoes, asks where she has been hiding, gets a literal answer, and tells his assistant to call Luca.




ACTIVE REFERENCES




@Image 1 — location reference: the room's geometry, its furniture and where that furniture stands, its materials and its window-light direction.




@Image 2 RAPHAEL: man, mid-50s, lean, head of the house. Standing, hands at his sides. Face, hair and suit are a 100% match to the reference.




@Image 3 LUCY: woman, 23, junior accountant. Loose, slightly messy mid-length hair falling to her shoulder blades, hair down and uncovered, no glasses on her face and no glasses anywhere on her body. Pale blue cardigan worn open over a light tank, dark checked pencil skirt, the same length as @Image 3, and the same black shoes and thin silver chain as that reference. Both arms are wrapped around a stack of glossy magazines held flat against her chest. Her hands are otherwise empty: nothing hangs from either elbow, nothing hangs from either shoulder, both forearms are free of straps of any kind. Face, hair and outfit are a 100% match to the reference.




@Image 4 REBECCA: woman, about 30, his assistant. The black dress, outfit details and loose brunette hair with a blunt fringe from @Image 4, carrying a slim clipboard. A 100% match to the reference.




HOW TO USE THE CHARACTER REFERENCES




Take from each character reference only the face, the hair and the clothing. Do not take the lighting, the background, the wall colour or the camera angle of any character reference. All three people are lit by this scene's own afternoon window light and stand in the room from @Image 1.




THE TABLE




Keep the long pale work table exactly where @Image 1 puts it: in the near foreground of the room on the screen-LEFT side, running away from the camera, with its scatter of magazines, a desk lamp, a vase of peonies and a corded telephone on it. Same position in the room, same angle, same objects on top of it as in the location reference. The table stays there for the whole clip and nothing about it changes.




THE MAGAZINES




The magazines in LUCY's arms are glossy fashion issues seen mostly edge-on as a stack. Where a cover shows, the paper is plain and empty: a photographed face and smooth blank paper where a title would sit, with no letters, words, numerals, logos or printed marks anywhere on any cover, spine or page. The magazines on the table are the same: photographs on blank paper. REBECCA's clipboard holds one sheet of plain unprinted paper.




LOCATION MAP




The showroom from @Image 1: pale walls, a pale bleached-oak floor, the cream paper runway strip down the middle of the room, tall arched windows with the evening city skyline beyond them along the far side, clothing racks, a dress form, a mirror.




RAPHAEL and LUCY stand 1.2 m apart out on the open floor in the middle of the room, deeper in than the table, inside a shaft of afternoon sun. Camera is on the east side of them for every segment. RAPHAEL is screen-LEFT facing screen-right. LUCY is screen-RIGHT facing screen-left.




REBECCA stands three steps behind RAPHAEL, further into the room on the screen-left side, holding her clipboard against her body. She is behind him and small in the frame whenever the shot is wide enough to include her.




The table is in the foreground on the screen-left side, between the camera and nobody — closer to the lens than all three of them.




FIRST FRAME AND SPATIAL BLOCKING




Frame one already holds both of them in a standing two-shot in profile: RAPHAEL at x 30%, LUCY at x 70%, three-quarter figures, eyes on each other. Behind RAPHAEL, deeper in the room, REBECCA standing with her clipboard, small and out of focus. Beyond them the bright arched windows.




FORMAT MODE




Aspect ratio 4:3, 1440 x 1080, landscape. Controlled eleven-segment dialogue sequence, total 30 seconds, real-time motion, no slow motion and no speed ramps. Segments 1 to 6 alternate by REVERSE CUT; segments 7 to 11 use HARD CUTs.




OPTICS




Segment 1: 47° standard normal, camera 4.5 m east of the pair at 1.4 m height, both sharp, REBECCA soft behind RAPHAEL.




Setup A, on LUCY (segments 2, 4): 29° short telephoto portrait, camera 4.5 m away just behind RAPHAEL's right shoulder; his shoulder and jaw sit in the foreground screen-left as a soft dark shape occupying no more than a quarter of the frame width. LUCY's eyes razor-sharp, the room behind her creamy bokeh.




Setup B, on RAPHAEL (segments 3, 5): 29° short telephoto portrait, camera 4.5 m away just behind LUCY's left shoulder; her hair and cardigan sit in the foreground screen-right as a soft shape occupying no more than a quarter of the frame width. RAPHAEL sharp, the room a warm blur behind him.




Setup C, on REBECCA (segment 6): 29° short telephoto portrait, camera 3.5 m from her where she stands on the open floor, REBECCA centred at x 50%, sharp, her eyes directed just past the lens toward the pair, the room soft behind her.




Segment 7: 47°, camera 4.5 m east, the two-shot frame again.




Segment 8: 47°, camera 5.5 m east, full figures, the room readable around them.




Segment 9: 47°, camera 4.5 m east, the two-shot frame again.




Segment 10: 29° short telephoto single on LUCY, camera 4.5 m east and stepped clear of RAPHAEL so no foreground shoulder enters the frame; LUCY at x 60%, head and shoulders, sharp.




Segment 11: 47°, camera 3 m from the table, framing REBECCA standing at it with the corded telephone, the table surface and its objects across the bottom of the frame, the room soft behind her.




CAMERA




Every segment is locked off on a tripod, horizon level, eye level 1.5 m. No handheld, no dolly, no zoom, no push-in.




ACTION TIMING




0.0s to 2.4s — segment 1, two-shot. He looks at her with open, unhurried interest, his head tilted a degree. She reads his face for the size of the trouble, arms tight around the stack. RAPHAEL, gently: "Have you ever been photographed?" (0.5s to 2.0s).




2.4s REVERSE CUT




2.4s to 5.0s — segment 2, setup A on LUCY. She thinks about it seriously, genuinely searching for the accurate answer (2.4s to 3.3s), then: "For my employee eye-dee." (3.4s to 4.7s).




5.0s REVERSE CUT




5.0s to 8.8s — segment 3, setup B on RAPHAEL. A soft breath of a laugh and a warm smile that arrives slowly and stays (5.0s to 5.8s). "I want you…" (5.9s to 6.6s). He holds her eyes through the pause (6.6s to 7.3s). "…to model for SOUL." (7.4s to 8.7s).




8.8s REVERSE CUT




8.8s to 10.2s — segment 4, setup A on LUCY. Her eyes widen a fraction: "Me?" (9.4s to 9.8s).




10.2s REVERSE CUT




10.2s to 13.8s — segment 5, setup B on RAPHAEL, both lines spoken on camera with his face fully in frame. Warmly and without hesitation: "Yes, Lucy." (10.4s to 11.1s). A short beat while he holds her eyes (11.1s to 11.6s). Then, plainly, so she cannot mistake it: "I want you to be the face of it." (11.7s to 13.6s). He finishes the sentence completely and holds still until 13.8s.




13.8s REVERSE CUT




13.8s to 15.8s — segment 6, setup C on REBECCA standing on the open floor with her clipboard. Nobody speaks at all in this segment: a silent reaction with only room tone under it. She is not against any of this and is not hostile; she simply cannot follow what is happening. Polite bewilderment: her eyes move between the two of them, two blinks, a small lift of the eyebrows, a shift of weight from one foot to the other. Her lips stay closed for the whole segment.




15.8s HARD CUT




15.8s to 18.8s — segment 7, two-shot. LUCY, apologetic, trying to correct a kind mistake: "But… I'm an accountant." (16.0s to 17.4s). RAPHAEL, amused and affectionate, not dismissive: "I've already heard that, darling." (17.6s to 18.8s).




18.8s HARD CUT




18.8s to 21.8s — segment 8, wider. He takes one unhurried pace back screen-left and looks at her from her hair to her shoes with open admiration (18.8s to 19.4s), a small laugh in the voice: "Where have you been hiding?" (19.5s to 20.6s). LUCY, literal and helpful: "The third floor." (20.8s to 21.7s).




21.8s HARD CUT




21.8s to 24.2s — segment 9, two-shot. He clears his throat softly, still smiling at her (21.8s to 22.4s), then turns his head back over his shoulder toward REBECCA behind him and lifts two fingers in a small, calm gesture (22.5s to 22.9s): "Get Luca." (23.0s to 23.9s). His eyes come straight back to LUCY and he holds still until 24.2s.




24.2s HARD CUT




24.2s to 25.6s — segment 10, single on LUCY. Her head stays where it is, still angled toward RAPHAEL; only her eyes move, following REBECCA across the room, one small blink, then coming back to him. She does not turn around and does not turn her head. Nobody speaks. Off camera we hear heels crossing the oak floor.




25.6s HARD CUT




25.6s to 30.0s — segment 11, at the table. This is the final shot of the clip, one unbroken continuous take with no cut inside it, running all the way to the last frame. REBECCA walks the last two steps up to the table and stops at it (25.6s to 26.3s), lays the clipboard flat on the table top (26.3s to 26.9s), lifts the corded handset (26.9s to 27.6s), presses the keypad with one finger, one button at a time, four separate visible presses (27.6s to 29.1s), raises the handset to her ear and waits, listening (29.1s to 30.0s). Her lips stay closed. The clip ends with her still listening.




REBECCA MOVEMENT RULE




For the first 24 seconds REBECCA stays where she is, standing on the open floor behind RAPHAEL with her clipboard; she does not walk anywhere during the conversation. She moves exactly once, on his order: she turns and crosses the room to the table in the foreground, which is the only place the telephone is, and makes the call standing at it. That single walk is the only time she changes position in the whole clip.




DIALOGUE PLACEMENT RULE




Every spoken line in this clip belongs entirely to one segment and is delivered on camera by the person speaking, with their face in frame and their lips moving. No line begins in one segment and finishes in another, and no line is heard while the speaker is out of frame. Each sentence starts and finishes completely inside its own shot at one steady close recording level, with no fade, no drop in volume and no trailing off at any cut. Segments 6, 10 and 11 contain no dialogue whatsoever.




ACTING TASK — RAPHAEL (warm, delighted, protective; the work is in his eyes):




MOTIVE: he has just seen the first true face all day and he does not want to frighten it away.




GOAL: make her understand she is not in trouble, and get her to yes before she can talk herself out of it.




OBSTACLE: she keeps apologising and answering literally, as if he has made an administrative error.




TACTIC: he keeps his voice low and kind and his body still, so nothing about him reads as a threat. He hunts both her eyes after each answer, looking for the expression he saw. Then he stops persuading and starts arranging — the step back is measuring, the glance over his shoulder is casting — but his eyes come back to her each time.




— "Have you ever been photographed?" — an invitation, not a test.




— "I want you… to model for SOUL." — said slowly, so she has room to hear it.




— "Yes, Lucy." — reassurance; her name is the whole point of the line.




— "I want you to be the face of it." — plain and certain, no persuasion left in it.




— "I've already heard that, darling." — affectionate, forgiving the objection.




— "Get Luca." — the decision, given softly, backwards, without really leaving her.




ACTING TASK — LUCY:




MOTIVE: she was sent from the third floor with papers and expects to be reprimanded.




GOAL: leave with her job; politely correct what she is sure is a misunderstanding.




OBSTACLE: he is not misunderstanding anything, and kindness is harder to answer than anger.




TACTIC: she searches his face for the catch, answers every question with the most accurate fact available, and grips the stack a little tighter each time he speaks. In segment 10 she follows REBECCA with her eyes to find out whether this is really happening — with her eyes only, because her head does not leave him.




— "For my employee eye-dee." — the exact truth, because exact answers are safe.




— "Me?" — checking whether anyone is joking.




— "The third floor." — the most accurate fact available.




ACTING TASK — REBECCA: she is not opposed to any of this and she is not hostile; she simply cannot follow what is happening. Politely bewildered while she listens: eyes moving between the two of them, two blinks, a small lift of the eyebrows, weight shifting from one foot to the other. Then she hears her cue and becomes pure competence — she crosses to the table and makes the call without a wasted movement.




(Safety: gazes engaged, natural blink cadence, listeners' mouths closed.)




PHYSICS




The magazine stack stays firmly clamped in both of LUCY's arms for the entire 30 seconds. The stack never slips, never shifts down, never slides, and nothing at all falls from her arms at any moment. Nothing is dropped by anyone in this scene. The stack rises and falls a few millimetres with her breathing only. Loose strands of her hair move with her head turns. His step back has real weight transfer, heel then toe. REBECCA's walk has real weight and real heel contact on the oak floor. The telephone handset lifts with a coiled cord that stretches and hangs.




The air in the room is completely still. Nothing is blowing, lifting, floating, flying or caught in a breeze.




LIGHTING




Afternoon sun through the tall arched windows is the only source. Faces are lit by soft bounce off the pale walls and floor, even and gentle from both sides, with a warm rim along hair and shoulders. Setup A has the window glow behind LUCY and soft fill on her face; setup B has the shaft raking RAPHAEL from screen-right; REBECCA is lit by the same daylight, a stop softer, her face clearly readable in both her shots. Exposure is honest: the windows are not blown out, the city skyline outside remains visible. No studio lamp, softbox, reflector, stand, flag, cable or crew is visible anywhere in the frame, in any corner, at any moment.




AUDIO




Voices close and clean, room tone low underneath them. RAPHAEL: low, dry, precise, unhurried, warm; English spoken with a French accent in every line. LUCY: soft, quick, mid-register, earnest. REBECCA: silent for the entire clip. Lips move only for the scripted lines. Every spoken line sits at the same close recording level from its first word to its last.




PRONUNCIATION: in LUCY's line "For my employee eye-dee." the two syllables are voiced exactly as written — "eye" as in the English word eye, then "dee" as in the letter D, with the stress on "dee". They are never merged into a single word and never spoken as any other word.




SFX: paper shifting against wool, his shoes on the floor, heels crossing oak, a clipboard set on the table, a handset lifted from its cradle, four soft keypad tones.




NO MUSIC: there is no music of any kind in this clip. No score, no soundtrack, no underscore, no piano, no strings, no pads, no ambient musical tone, no hum, no drone and no rhythm. The only sounds are the voices, the room tone and the listed effects.




POSITIVE LOCKS




RAPHAEL stays screen-left facing screen-right and LUCY stays screen-right facing screen-left in every segment they share. The table stays exactly where the location reference puts it, in the foreground on the screen-left side, for all 30 seconds. REBECCA stands behind RAPHAEL until his order and then walks to that table, and this is the only movement she makes. LUCY never turns around. Segments 2 to 5 use only the two over-the-shoulder setups. LUCY has no glasses on her face and no glasses in her hands at any moment. LUCY's forearms and shoulders stay clear: nothing is slung, strapped or hanging from them, and both arms stay wrapped around the magazines. Only the quoted lines are spoken and nothing else is said. Clean picture with no on-screen text, no subtitles, no captions, no titles, no watermark and no lettering on any surface, garment, wall, page or object in the room.




LOOK: mid-2000s 35mm feature-film photography. Fine Kodak Vision2 negative grain, warm-timed print: honey-caramel skin and wood, clean daylight at the windows, gently lifted blacks, glossy fashion-magazine saturation on dark fabric, chrome and lips. Light diffusion-filter glow: highlights and chrome bloom softly, skin reads smooth, gentle halation on bright edges. Rectilinear lenses, straight verticals. Shallow depth of field with creamy round bokeh wherever a face is the subject. Real 24 fps motion blur. Fully photoreal live action, mid-2000s New York: the only telephones in this world are corded landlines and slim silver flip phones.
