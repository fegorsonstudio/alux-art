# Their example 3 — from the Higgsfield blog (SOUL Angel)

## The lesson around it


Scene 3: the escalation

In scene three, Raphael wants to find out who Lucy is, so he gets up and walks over to her. This is the escalation, where you need an unexpected turn: new characters show up, the conflict grows, things happen that the viewer doesn't see coming.

And here's the biggest secret behind viral short dramas: they let you catch every little reaction. The tension comes from how differently the two characters read the situation. Lucy thinks she's in trouble. Raphael thinks he's found someone interesting. So describe both performances, and spell out the movement too:

SCENE CONTEXT




The accountant, on her knees among scattered papers, pushes her loose hair out of her 




face and starts gathering her pages; the head of the house, a Frenchman, weary at the 




corner of his table, notices her and his boredom turns into interest; he asks his 




assistant who she is and is timidly offered the option of firing her; he gets up and 




goes to the girl. His polished shoes stop in front of her; she apologizes to the floor; 




he gently stops her apology and asks her name; she tells him; he praises it warmly, 




offers his hand and helps her up. Face to face, he asks if she has ever modeled.

ACTING TASK — RAPHAEL: DIRECTION: get through another empty day. MOTIVE: everything 




around him is fake and he is tired of it; the girl on the floor is the first real thing 




he has seen. GOAL: find out who she is without frightening her away. OBSTACLE: she is 




scared, folded around a pile of paper, her face hidden behind her hair, expecting 




punishment. TACTIC: the question to REBECCA is thrown sideways while his eyes stay on 




the girl; he ignores the offer to fire her and simply gets up and goes; at the floor he 




stops her apology softly, like calming a startled bird; he gives her name back to her 




as a compliment, pronounced the French way, so she feels safe; he offers his hand 




instead of an order and lifts her carefully; only when she is standing and calm does 




he ask the question he crossed the room for.




ACTING TASK — REBECCA: DIRECTION: the same. MOTIVE: his mood is her job and she is afraid 




of him. GOAL: guess what he wants and give it to him first. OBSTACLE: she cannot read 




him and does not know the girl's name. TACTIC: she stalls with "uh", gives the only 




fact she has, and offers the standard solution softly, like a question, ready to take 




it back; when he walks away without a word she understands she guessed wrong.




ACTING TASK — LUCY: DIRECTION: nobody makes a scene. MOTIVE: she is new and afraid of 




losing the job. GOAL: clean up before anyone judges her. OBSTACLE: his shoes will not 




move. TACTIC: she apologizes to the floor, hiding behind her hair so she does not have 




to see the verdict; his gentle voice disarms her, so she looks up and answers her name 




simply; the compliment and the offered hand confuse her into trust, and she takes it; 




the last question is so far from what she feared that she has no answer for it.




(Safety: gazes engaged, natural blink cadence, listeners' mouths closed.)
