# Their example 1 — from the Higgsfield blog (SOUL Angel)

## The lesson around it

A location map and a first frame
The same voice everywhere:
Scene 1: the hook and the context

The first scene covers two parts of the drama: the hook and the context. The hook starts with the peak of the scene: an emotional outburst, a conflict, a fall. The viewer sees the loudest moment without knowing how it got there, so they keep watching to find out. 
In the context, keep the emotional swings coming every 20 to 30 seconds: a starting point, then hope, then another hit, then a chance.

SCENE CONTEXT




Late-afternoon casting at the house. The head of the house, a Frenchman, sits weary at the corner of his table in the red chair; his assistant stands at his shoulder and marks everything on her clipboard; the models wait in a line on the paper runway and, one by one, walk the last meters to the mark and strike the same pose. The first one can barely walk in her heels: she teeters toward the mark with the queue standing behind her, he watches with a raised eyebrow and murmurs a sarcastic "Eww. What is this?", and then, planting her foot for the pose, she catches her heel on the runway's edge and goes down. He winces for her and softens at once, sends her off gently. Three more walk the same walk and hit the identical pose, identical timing, identical smile, and with each one his "Next" comes quieter and flatter; to him they are one girl. He stops the last one a stride short of the mark and asks whether she has already been in; she says it is her first time; he says softly that he swears he has seen her, and sends her away with the same word. Then, to nobody, almost a whisper: they all have the same face.

MODEL ONE reaches the mark on her last stride and plants her foot to shift into the pose (3.8s to 4.6s); her heel catches the lifted edge of the paper runway, the paper bunches under her shoe, her ankle rolls out (4.6s to 5.0s); she pitches sideways toward the windows, arms flying out, and goes down onto her hip and both palms across the runway's west edge (5.0s to 5.6s); she stays down, head bowed, hair over her face (5.6s to 6.0s); in the line behind her two heads turn, nobody moves; REBECCA stays at the table. The sarcasm falls off his face; he winces for her, a small pained breath in, the hand slides down from his temple to the tabletop, he leans forward an inch, the beginning of a kind smile (6.0s to 6.4s); very soft, tender, almost apologetic, to the girl on the floor: "Oh — it's all right. 15.0s HARD CUT

20.8s HARD CUT

The smile lets go; one blink; she turns her head away screen-right and steps out of frame toward the door (21.6s to 22.3s); heels click away.




22.3s HARD CUT




22.3s to 25.8s — RAPHAEL close-up. He closes the portfolio on the tabletop with a soft slap of his left hand (22.5s to 23.1s); his eyes drop; whatever was left of the edge drains away and leaves only tiredness; the hand comes back up and the fingertips settle at his temple; without looking at REBECCA, barely above a whisper, half to himself: "Bon. Same face. All of them." (23.6s to 25.2s). Hold on his face to 25.8s; REBECCA gives a small nod and ticks the page one more time.




ACTING TASK — RAPHAEL (fully invested; the work is in his eyes): DIRECTION: get through another empty day without anyone making a scene. MOTIVE: everything around him is fake and he is tired of it; he is a kind man who hates this part of the job, sarcasm is his reflex and kindness is his nature, and today the sameness is wearing the kindness thin one girl at a time. GOAL: find one thing in one of these girls that was not rehearsed. OBSTACLE: the walk is perfect, the pose is perfect, and every face is the same face. TACTIC: he does everything from the chair, mannered and unhurried, fingertips at the temple like a man watching a clock; he never raises his voice — his irritation shows only as the voice getting quieter, flatter and slower; the "Eww" is a murmured reflex he is ashamed of a second later, which is why the softness that follows the fall is real — he calms her like a startled bird; each "Next" after that is shorter and quieter than the last; with MODEL FOUR the tiredness comes out as gentle certainty — he would swear he has seen her — and since nothing can prove it either way he ends it with the same soft word; the last line is not to her or to REBECCA but to the day itself, and it comes out as a whisper.




ACTING TASK — REBECCA: DIRECTION: the same. MOTIVE: his mood is her job and she is afraid of him. GOAL: guess what he wants, keep the line moving, and have a mark on the page for everything, so nothing can be her fault later. OBSTACLE: the list says the girl is new and he says she is not. TACTIC: she never leaves the table; she writes down the wobble, the fall and every "Next"; as his voice gets quieter she stands a little straighter; at his question she checks the list twice, shakes her head only to herself, marks the page anyway and keeps her mouth closed because she is not sure which of them is right; at the end she agrees with a nod because agreeing is safest.




ACTING TASK — THE MODELS: MODEL ONE: DIRECTION: the same. MOTIVE: first real casting, borrowed heels a size too high, five girls watching her back. GOAL: reach the mark and hit the pose without looking at her feet. OBSTACLE: her ankles. TACTIC: she fights the shoes step by step, stays down a beat too long after the fall, then leaves without looking at the table or the line. THE LINE: they wait like a queue at a counter, still, eyes forward, two heads turning at the fall and turning back. MODEL TWO and MODEL THREE walk and pose cleanly, mechanically, with nothing behind the smile. MODEL FOUR: DIRECTION: the same. MOTIVE: the twelfth casting this week. GOAL: get her two seconds and leave. TACTIC: she holds the smile like a held breath and answers him flat, because arguing has never once helped; the "Next" lands in her eyes for exactly one blink before she turns.




(Safety: gazes engaged, natural blink cadence, listeners' mouths closed.)
